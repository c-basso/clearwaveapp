How to Fix a Blown Speaker — and Check If It's Really Blown

By Vladimir Ivakhnenko, developer of Clear Wave · Updated 2026-09-29

Quick answer

First, check it isn't just water or dust: run 2–3 water eject cycles, brush the grille, and test again. A truly blown speaker (torn membrane or damaged voice coil) crackles or buzzes at every volume and can't be fixed with software — it needs a speaker replacement. Many "blown" phone speakers turn out to be clogged.

"Blown" means the speaker is physically damaged — usually from being driven too hard, a drop, or corrosion. But water and debris produce almost the same symptoms. Before you pay for a repair, spend two minutes ruling those out.

How to test and fix a "blown" speaker

  1. Play clean audio at 50% volume. If it sounds fine at 50% and only distorts when loud, it's probably not blown.
  2. Eject water and debris. Speaker down, 70–80% volume, low-frequency water eject tone for 30–60 seconds, 2–3 times.
  3. Brush the grille. Soft, dry toothbrush — never pins or needles.
  4. Sweep test tones. Play tones from low to high. A blown speaker buzzes across many frequencies, not just one.
  5. Compare speakers. Use a left/right test. If one side is clean and the other rattles at every level, that speaker is damaged.
  6. Replace if confirmed. A phone speaker replacement is a routine repair at Apple or a reputable shop.

  1. Clean first. Run a Water Eject session to rule out water and dust.
  2. Stereo Test. Play Left and Right channels separately and compare.
  3. Tone Generator sweep. Swipe slowly from low to high frequency and note where it buzzes.
  4. dB Meter. Compare loudness between speakers — a big drop on one side confirms a problem.

Signs your speaker is blown

  • Crackling or fuzz at low volume, not just loud volume.
  • A constant rattle on bass notes that doesn't improve after drying for 24 hours.
  • No sound at all from one speaker while the other works.
  • Problems started right after a hard drop.

Myths about fixing blown speakers

"A special frequency can fix a blown speaker." No. Tones can move water and dust, but they can't repair a torn cone or burnt coil. "Glue or tape can fix it." Not on a phone — speakers are sealed modules. If yours is truly blown, replacement is the fix.

Questions

Can a blown phone speaker fix itself?

No. But a speaker that sounds blown because of water often recovers once it dries or after water eject cycles.

How much does it cost to fix a blown iPhone speaker?

It depends on the model and where you repair it. Check Apple's repair pricing page or a local repair shop; AppleCare+ may cover it.
