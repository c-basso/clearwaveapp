Water Eject App for iPhone: What It Does and How to Use One

By Vladimir Ivakhnenko, developer of Clear Wave · Updated 2026-09-29

Quick answer

A water eject app plays a tuned low-frequency sound through your iPhone speaker. The vibrating membrane pushes trapped water out of the grille in 30–60 seconds. A good app also lets you test the result — stereo left/right check, test tones and a dB meter — so you know when the speaker is really clear.

iPhones don't have a built-in water eject button (Apple Watch does — it's called Water Lock). A water eject app fills that gap. Instead of a website tone that you have to keep open in a browser, an app plays calibrated sound sessions offline, lets you repeat them, and gives you tools to check whether the speaker sounds normal again.

How to use a water eject app

  1. Download the app. Install a water eject app such as Clear Wave from the App Store. It works offline once installed.
  2. Remove the case and dry the outside. Wipe the phone and speaker grille with a lint-free cloth.
  3. Turn volume up to 70–80% and face the speaker down. Gravity plus vibration moves the water out.
  4. Start the water eject session. Let it run until it finishes. Wipe away any droplets on the grille.
  5. Test and repeat. Play a test tone or music. If it still sounds muffled, run 1–2 more sessions.

What to look for in a water eject app

  • Low-frequency sessions (around 150–200 Hz) — high-pitched beeps move very little water.
  • Volume control — you should never need 100% volume.
  • A way to verify — stereo test and test tones tell you whether the fix worked.
  • Offline and private — Clear Wave's sessions run on your device; it declares no data linked to your identity on the App Store.
  • Honest limits — no app can fix a physically blown speaker.

Does a water eject app actually work?

For water sitting in the speaker grille and the small cavity behind it — the most common cause of muffled sound after rain, a shower or a splash — yes. You can usually see droplets come out. It won't help with water inside the logic board or a damaged speaker. Read more: Does water eject actually work?
